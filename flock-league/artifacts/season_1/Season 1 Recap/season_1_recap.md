# Flock League Season 1: From Knockout to Champion

*Narrative recap script*

## The beginning

Season 1 began with twelve Team Owners, a knockout game for draft position, and a simple promise: one of them would become the first Flock League champion, while someone else would finish as the Ultimate Loser.

Tom earned the first pick. Thomas, Zach, Austin, Avery, Danny, Mason, Bush, Badake, Nathan, Sal, and Joe followed. The league used full-PPR scoring, deep starting lineups, kickers, and defenses. But the draft board was only the starting point. Almost immediately, the owners began reshaping their teams.

Mason and Danny completed the league's first official trade before Week 1, exchanging Nico Collins and Trey Benson for Jaylen Waddle and Rhamondre Stevenson. It established the personality of the season: nobody was going to wait patiently for the roster they drafted to work. Every injury, bad Sunday, or convincing sales pitch could produce another deal.

That impatience became even more important once the games started.

## Fast starts and immediate trouble

Week 1 belonged to Tom, Mason, Danny, Badake, Austin, and Bush. Badake led the league with 148.30 points, while Danny and Austin also cleared 140. At the other end, Nathan and Thomas immediately encountered the kind of injury luck that can change an entire fantasy season. Nathan lost Puka Nacua to injured reserve, and Thomas entered Monday night without Christian McCaffrey.

Nathan responded by making one of the biggest trades of the year. He sent Justin Jefferson, Aaron Jones, Tank Bigsby, and Brock Bowers to Avery for Kyren Williams, Michael Pittman, Tony Pollard, and Dallas Goedert. Avery emerged with Jefferson beside Amon-Ra St. Brown and renamed his team "the betting favorite." Nathan gained depth, but the Jefferson deal—and later the decision to move Brock Bowers—would remain part of the story of a season in which he was constantly trying to repair the previous week.

Mason was just as aggressive. He acquired injured Hollywood Brown and Javonte Williams in a deal that drew immediate criticism, then spent much of the season cycling through quarterbacks, receivers, defenses, and trade proposals. His roster seemed to absorb one blow after another: Brown went to injured reserve, Anthony Richardson was hurt shortly after Mason acquired him, Jonathan Taylor left a game injured, and several receiver options disappeared. Yet Mason kept winning.

Danny had the cleanest opening. After three weeks he was 3-0, first in the standings, and the league's leading scorer. His early roster moves looked sharp, and he was willing to spend heavily when he saw an opportunity, including a $37 bid for Carson Steele. For the first month, Danny looked like the standard everyone else had to catch.

Then Nathan handed him his first loss, 125.68-83.34, and the season compressed almost overnight.

## The league tightens

After Week 4, Danny, Zach, and Mason were tied at 3-1, with five more owners sitting at 2-2. There was no longer one obvious favorite.

Injuries helped drive the change. Danny lost Rashee Rice as a major option and later spent time without Nico Collins. Mason continued to patch holes. Sal struggled to find healthy running backs. Thomas's roster had already been shaped by McCaffrey's absence. Nathan was dealing with the consequences of the Nacua injury and an 0-2 start.

Zach was the owner who best converted the chaos into momentum. He won Tucker Kraft for $31 FAAB, kept trading, and assembled a roster that could survive turnover. A blockbuster sent Brandon Aiyuk, D'Onta Foreman, and Jalen Tolbert to Bush, while Zach received Amari Cooper, Kareem Hunt, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Bucky Irving, and $30 FAAB. Later, Zach and Sal exchanged another large collection of players, with Jonathan Taylor and Terry McLaurin among the pieces moving to Zach. Zach also acquired Mike Evans and Chris Olave from Thomas.

Not every move aged perfectly. Chris Godwin was lost after helping complete a remarkable Week 7 comeback, and Olave suffered another head injury after changing teams. But Zach kept accumulating options, and the wins kept coming.

By Week 6, Zach had won five straight and stood alone at 5-1. A week later he was 6-1. After Week 9, he was 8-1.

That run made Zach the central figure of the regular season. Everyone measured a contender by whether that team could keep pace with him.

## Rivalries, reversals, and the middle of the pack

While Zach pulled ahead, the rest of the league became defined by narrow margins.

No result captured that better than Avery against Mason in Week 5. Mason initially appeared to win 157.16-156.02. Then Sleeper assigned a final-play fumble to Pat Freiermuth and removed two points from Mason's lineup. The correction gave Avery the victory. Team names changed in real time as both owners celebrated, panicked, and celebrated again. What looked like a strange early-season footnote eventually became part of the champion's path.

Avery's season never followed a straight line. He began 2-0, built around the Jefferson blockbuster, lost a week despite scoring 157, crushed Danny with 178.04, and later finished the regular season only 7-7. His roster could look overwhelming one week and vulnerable the next. That inconsistency kept him out of the regular-season spotlight, but it also concealed how dangerous his best lineup could be.

Austin was steadier. He opened 2-0, scored 184.14 in a Week 8 win over Nathan, and spent much of the year near the top of the standings. He also acquired Christian McCaffrey, only to lose him again before the playoffs. Austin still finished 8-6 and earned the third seed, but his season became another example of how quickly an apparent roster upgrade could turn into an injury problem.

Badake also spent much of the season in contention. He produced the highest Week 1 score, climbed to 5-2, and repeatedly found useful short-term plays, including Ray Davis in the Week 6 comeback over Nathan. But the second half was less forgiving. By the final regular-season week, Badake needed to beat Zach and could not. The 104.76-97.74 loss ended his championship hopes.

Joe traveled in the opposite direction. His season looked lost early, and he became a frequent trade partner while trying to rebuild. Then the results turned. Joe upset Mason, put together a four-game winning streak, acquired Josh Allen from Sal, and entered Week 14 with one last route into the playoffs. He beat Danny 132.38-130.52 and received the other results he needed. From the edge of elimination, Joe became the fifth seed.

Tom remained dangerous without ever establishing the same momentum. He made a major deal with Joe for Lamar Jackson, Jahmyr Gibbs, and Marvin Harrison Jr., produced several strong weeks, and entered the final stretch as part of the enormous 6-7 cluster. His season ended on the wrong side of Mason's 147.70-145.06 Week 14 comeback. Fewer than three points separated Tom from a very different postseason.

## The owners fighting uphill

Sal's season was a continuous attempt to trade his way out of trouble. He started 0-2, fell to 1-4, and eventually reached 3-7. He acquired and moved quarterbacks, rebuilt his receiver group, and completed a late wave of deals involving Bo Nix, Drake London, and Josh Allen. The Allen trade sent Allen and a larger package to Joe while Sal received Sam Darnold, Nick Chubb, Courtland Sutton, and Rico Dowdle.

The changes finally produced momentum. Sal won three straight and scored roughly 166 points to beat Avery in Week 13. He reached Week 14 at 6-7 with a legitimate playoff path. Austin stopped the comeback, 142.52-136.24, leaving Sal just outside the championship bracket. Sal later beat Nathan on the Toilet Bowl side and escaped the final punishment matchup.

Danny's fall was more dramatic because of how high he started. The 3-0 league leader lost Rashee Rice, played through Nico Collins's absence, dealt with Breece Hall uncertainty, and endured a stretch of four losses in five games. Danny recovered to 6-5 and remained alive until the final regular-season matchup, but the two-point loss to Joe eliminated him. He then defeated Badake in the first Toilet Bowl round and secured safety from the Ultimate Loser race.

Nathan's season became a catalogue of painful choices. Nacua's injury hit immediately. He traded Jefferson and Bowers, narrowly missed waiver targets, and later identified benching Jerry Jeudy during a huge performance as one of his worst decisions. Even so, Nathan fought back from 0-2, handed Danny his first loss, and stayed competitive late enough to affect the playoff race. After missing the championship bracket, he survived to the final Toilet Bowl matchup against Bush.

Bush spent much of the year near the bottom while repeatedly trying to rebuild through trades and waivers. The Aiyuk deal gave him a major name but eventually became another injury story. Later moves brought Calvin Ridley and DJ Moore, and individual weeks offered hope, but a four-game losing streak left him last at 4-8 entering Week 13. Bush reached the final punishment matchup, where quarterback availability became decisive.

Thomas had the strangest route of anyone who reached the championship bracket. McCaffrey's Week 1 absence immediately put him behind. His 6-8 regular-season record looked ordinary, but he finished third in points scored and produced some of the year's largest totals, including 188.92 in a win over Badake. Most importantly, Thomas ended Zach's eight-game winning streak in Week 10. The record said sixth seed. The scoring profile said nobody should want to face him.

## Fourteen weeks to decide six places

The final regular-season race was almost impossibly crowded.

Zach had already secured the No. 1 seed. Mason, despite ranking only seventh in points scored, had survived injuries, trades, a stat-correction loss, and constant roster turnover. He won when he needed to, finished 9-5, and secured the second bye.

Austin finished third at 8-6. Avery and Joe both finished 7-7. Thomas claimed the final playoff place at 6-8. Badake, Danny, Sal, Tom, Nathan, and Bush missed the championship bracket, several of them separated from Thomas by little more than one result or a tiebreaker.

That left six contenders: Zach, Mason, Austin, Avery, Joe, and Thomas.

## The playoffs

Zach and Mason waited with byes while the opening round paired Avery with Joe and Thomas with Austin.

Joe's recovery season ended against Avery. Austin's steady season ended when Thomas won 157.04-144.50. The sixth seed, already short on healthy options, advanced to face Mason. Avery moved on to face the regular season's dominant owner, Zach.

The semifinals overturned the expected championship.

Thomas entered without David Montgomery and Alvin Kamara, used Patrick Taylor and Alexander Mattison in his flex positions, and then produced his best performance when it mattered most. He defeated Mason 189.04-143.82.

In the other semifinal, Avery eliminated Zach. The owner who had finished 7-7 had beaten the 11-3 top seed. Zach's regular-season dominance still earned him a third-place opportunity, but the championship would belong to someone else.

Zach responded by beating Mason 217.58-149.08 in the third-place game. Mason finished fourth after a season in which he repeatedly survived the consequences of his own aggression. Zach finished third after setting the regular-season standard.

That left Avery and Thomas.

Thomas entered the final with his running backs depleted: Chuba Hubbard was on injured reserve, Alvin Kamara and David Montgomery were out, and Jerome Ford was questionable. Avery received an early eruption from Tee Higgins, who scored 40.10 points and three touchdowns on Thursday night. Thomas never recovered.

The displayed final was 166.38-109.38.

Avery—the owner who was 7-7, who had survived the Freiermuth stat correction, who had traded aggressively for Justin Jefferson, and who had spent the season oscillating between favorite and disappointment—became the first Flock League champion.

## The other final

The championship was only half of Week 17's stakes.

Nathan and Bush met in the final Toilet Bowl matchup. Nathan attacked the quarterback pool, adding Justin Herbert, Drake Maye, and Matthew Stafford in an effort to restrict Bush's choices. Bush ultimately started Aaron Rodgers, who scored only 2.48 points, after also sitting Patrick Mahomes for Anthony Richardson during the week.

Nathan won by 15 points. Under the league's loser-bracket rules, that left Bush as Season 1's Ultimate Loser and the owner carrying the punishment into the following season. Nathan did not escape the jokes—he was called the league's "12th-best team" and appeared with a toilet seat and turkey costume—but he escaped the final loss.

## Where every Team Owner finished

**Avery** won the championship. His season moved from a 2-0 start and the Justin Jefferson blockbuster through wild inconsistency, a 7-7 finish, and then three postseason wins over Joe, Zach, and Thomas.

**Thomas** finished second. He turned a 6-8 record and a heavily injured roster into upset wins over Austin and Mason before running out of healthy options against Avery.

**Zach** finished third. He was the defining regular-season force, went 11-3, earned the No. 1 seed, and survived major injuries and constant trades before Avery ended his title run.

**Mason** finished fourth. No owner generated more trade talk, roster churn, side bets, or chaos. Despite injuries and the season's strangest stat-correction loss, he earned the No. 2 seed before falling to Thomas and Zach.

**Austin** finished as the third seed but lost to Thomas in the opening playoff round. His strong, steady regular season was undermined late by losing Christian McCaffrey.

**Joe** completed one of the season's best recoveries, won his way into the fifth seed on the final day, and then lost to Avery in the opening round.

**Badake** spent much of the year in the playoff picture, rising as high as 5-2, but faded late and was eliminated by Zach in Week 14. After losing to Danny in the Toilet Bowl, he escaped before the final punishment matchup.

**Danny** went from the undefeated early leader to the wrong side of the playoff line. Injuries and a midseason losing streak erased his advantage, and the narrow Week 14 loss to Joe ended his title hopes. He beat Badake to secure Toilet Bowl safety.

**Sal** nearly completed the comeback of the year. He climbed from 3-7 to 6-7 through a trade-heavy three-game winning streak, but lost his final playoff chance against Austin. He later escaped the Toilet Bowl before the final.

**Tom** remained in the crowded race until Week 14. His two-point loss to Mason eliminated him from the championship bracket, but he avoided the final punishment matchup.

**Nathan** spent the season recovering from injuries, trades, missed waivers, and lineup regrets. He missed the playoffs, survived the Toilet Bowl, and beat Bush by 15 in the final despite ending the year as one of the league's favorite targets for jokes.

**Bush** endured the hardest finish. Injuries and a late losing streak pushed him to the bottom, and Nathan's quarterback-blocking strategy helped decide the Toilet Bowl. Bush became the inaugural Ultimate Loser.

## Closing

Season 1 did not reward the best regular-season record. It did not reward the quietest manager, the safest drafter, or the owner who avoided every mistake.

It rewarded the team that survived long enough to peak at exactly the right time.

Zach controlled the regular season. Mason survived the chaos. Thomas made the underdog run. But Avery won the games that decided the title.

The first name in Flock League history belongs to Avery: Season 1 champion.
